## 6. WHAT IS COTANGENT?

H2: Understanding the Cotangent Ratio

Introduce the formula:

\[
\cot(A)=\frac{b}{a}
\]

Explain each component:

- \(\cot(A)\) means the cotangent of angle \(A\).
- \(b\) is the adjacent side.

Explain that cotangent compares the length next to the reference angle with the length across from it.

### H3: Cotangent as the Reciprocal of Tangent

Begin with the tangent formula:

\[
\tan(A)=\frac{a}{b}
\]

Explain that taking the reciprocal reverses the fraction:

\[
\frac{1}{\tan(A)}=\frac{b}{a}
\]

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{\cot(A)=\frac{1}{\tan(A)}=\frac{b}{a}}
\]

Explain why the numerator and denominator change places.

Use the example:

\[
\tan(A)=\frac{3}{4}=0.75
\]

Then:

\[
\cot(A)=\frac{1}{0.75}=\frac{4}{3}\approx1.3333
\]

Explain why both ratios describe the same triangle from reciprocal perspectives.

**Important:** Explain that cotangent is the reciprocal of tangent, not the inverse tangent function. The notation \(\tan^{-1}\) commonly represents inverse tangent, while \(1/\tan(A)\) represents reciprocal tangent.

### H3: Understanding Cotangent as Run Divided by Rise

Explain the practical interpretation of cotangent.

When angle \(A\) is measured from the horizontal:

- Adjacent side \(b\) can represent horizontal run.
- Opposite side \(a\) can represent vertical rise.

Therefore:

\[
\cot(A)=\frac{\text{Horizontal run}}{\text{Vertical rise}}
\]

Explain how this differs from tangent, which is rise divided by run.

Clarify that this interpretation depends on the selected reference angle and the geometry of the situation.

### H3: What Does a Cotangent Value Mean?

Explain these three cases.

**Cotangent greater than 1**

The adjacent side is longer than the opposite side. The reference angle is less than \(45^\circ\).

**Cotangent equal to 1**

The adjacent and opposite sides are equal. The reference angle is \(45^\circ\).

**Cotangent between 0 and 1**

The opposite side is longer than the adjacent side. The reference angle is greater than \(45^\circ\) and less than \(90^\circ\).

Use clear numerical examples for each case.

## 12. HOW TO FIND AN ANGLE FROM COTANGENT

H2: How to Find an Angle When Cotangent Is Known

\[
\boxed{A=\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{\cot(A)}\right)}
\]

Equivalently:

\[
\boxed{A=\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)}

Final answer:
