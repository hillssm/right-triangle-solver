## 5. MATHEMATICAL DEFINITIONS AND CONSISTENT NOTATION

Use one consistent notation throughout the article.

Consider a right triangle with:

- a = first leg.
- b = second leg.
- c = hypotenuse.

The hypotenuse is opposite the 90-degree angle.

### A. PYTHAGOREAN THEOREM

\[
\boxed{a^2+b^2=c^2}
\]

Explain:

- a and b are the two perpendicular sides.
- c is the longest side.
- The squares of the legs add to the square of the hypotenuse.
- The equation is valid for every right triangle.
- A Pythagorean triple is a special case where all three side lengths are positive integers.

### B. DEFINITION OF A PYTHAGOREAN TRIPLE

A Pythagorean triple is an ordered or unordered set of three positive integers satisfying:

\[
a^2+b^2=c^2
\]

Use the standard notation:

\[
(a,b,c)
\]

Example:

\[
(3,4,5)
\]

Verification:

\[
9+16=25
\]

Explain why all three values must be positive integers for the set to qualify as a Pythagorean triple.

### C. EUCLID'S FORMULA

Introduce:

\[
\boxed{a=m^2-n^2}
\]

\[
\boxed{b=2mn}
\]

\[
\boxed{c=m^2+n^2}
\]

Where:

- m is the larger positive integer.
- n is the smaller positive integer.
- m > n > 0.

Explain how these equations produce a Pythagorean triple.

### D. PROOF OF EUCLID'S FORMULA

Start with:

\[
a=m^2-n^2
\]

\[
b=2mn
\]

Calculate:

\[
a^2+b^2=(m^2-n^2)^2+(2mn)^2
\]

Expand:

\[
=m^4-2m^2n^2+n^4+4m^2n^2
\]

Combine:

\[
=m^4+2m^2n^2+n^4
\]

Factor:

\[
=(m^2+n^2)^2
\]

Since:

\[
c=m^2+n^2
\]

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{a^2+b^2=c^2}
\]

Explain the proof step by step in accessible language.

### E. PRIMITIVE PYTHAGOREAN TRIPLES

A primitive triple is one where:

\[
\gcd(a,b,c)=1
\]

This means the three side lengths have no common positive integer factor greater than 1.

For Euclid's formula, a primitive triple is generated when:

1. m and n are coprime.
2. m and n have opposite parity.

Explain that these conditions are both necessary for a primitive triple from this formula.

Examples:

- (3, 4, 5) is primitive.
- (5, 12, 13) is primitive.
- (8, 15, 17) is primitive.
- (6, 8, 10) is not primitive.
- (9, 12, 15) is not primitive.

### F. NON-PRIMITIVE PYTHAGOREAN TRIPLES

A non-primitive triple is a positive integer triple whose three values share a common factor greater than 1.

Example:

\[
(3,4,5)
\]

Multiply every side by 2:

\[
(6,8,10)
\]

Multiply every side by 3:

\[
(9,12,15)
\]

Explain:

- Why both remain valid triples.
- Why neither scaled example is primitive.
- How to find the common factor.
- How dividing by the common factor recovers the primitive triple.

### G. SCALING A PYTHAGOREAN TRIPLE

For any positive integer k:

\[
\boxed{ka,\ kb,\ kc}
\]

is another Pythagorean triple whenever (a,b,c) is one.

Explain:

\[
(ka)^2+(kb)^2=(kc)^2
\]

\[
k^2a^2+k^2b^2=k^2c^2
\]

Factor:

\[
k^2(a^2+b^2)=k^2c^2
\]

Since the original triple satisfies the theorem, the scaled triple does too.

Important distinction:

A positive integer multiplier preserves the whole-number property. An arbitrary positive real multiplier preserves a right triangle's proportions but may not produce integer side lengths.

### H. PRIMITIVE TRIPLES AND EUCLID'S PARAMETERS

Explain the parameter conditions carefully.

A primitive triple is generated when:

\[
\gcd(m,n)=1
\]

and m and n have opposite parity.

Examples:

**Example 1:**

m = 2, n = 1.

They are coprime and have opposite parity.

Result: 3-4-5, primitive.

**Example 2:**

m = 3, n = 2.

They are coprime and have opposite parity.

Result: 5-12-13, primitive.

**Example 3:**

m = 3, n = 1.

They are coprime but both odd.

Result: 8-6-10, non-primitive.

**Example 4:**

m = 4, n = 2.

They share a common factor of 2.

Result: 12-16-20, non-primitive.

Do not claim that every m,n pair produces a primitive triple.
