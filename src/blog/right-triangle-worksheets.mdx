## Review These Right Triangle Rules Before You Begin

### Identifying the Legs and Hypotenuse

Every right triangle has exactly one 90° angle. The side opposite that angle is the **hypotenuse**, always the longest side. The two sides that form the right angle are the **legs**. On any diagram, find the right angle first; the side facing it names itself.

![A right triangle with its two legs and hypotenuse labelled](/images/blog/right-triangle-anatomy.svg)

### Pythagorean Theorem

**a² + b² = c²**, where a and b are the legs and c is the hypotenuse. Missing hypotenuse: c = √(a² + b²). Missing leg: b = √(c² − a²). The theorem in full, with its formula family, is in our [Pythagorean theorem formula](/blog/pythagorean-theorem-formula/) guide.

### Area of a Right Triangle

**Area = ½ × base × height**, and in a right triangle the two legs are the base and height, because they stand perpendicular to each other. No extra construction needed.

### Perimeter of a Right Triangle

**Perimeter = a + b + c.** Add all three sides once you have them; our [perimeter guide](/blog/how-to-find-the-perimeter-of-a-right-triangle/) works the cases where a side is missing first.

### Special Right Triangle Ratios

**30-60-90: 1 : √3 : 2**, read as short leg (opposite 30°), long leg (opposite 60°), hypotenuse (opposite 90°). **45-45-90: 1 : 1 : √2**, the two legs equal, the hypotenuse leg × √2. The [30-60-90 guide](/blog/30-60-90-triangle/) and the [angles guide](/blog/angles-in-a-right-triangle/) go deeper on both.

## Three Solved Examples Before You Practice

### Example 1: Find the Hypotenuse

**Given:** legs of 6 cm and 8 cm. **Method:** both legs known, hypotenuse missing, so add the squares. c² = 6² + 8² = 36 + 64 = 100; c = √100 = **10 cm**.

![A 6-8-10 right triangle with the hypotenuse calculation shown](/images/blog/right-triangle-worked-example.svg)

### Example 2: Calculate the Missing Leg

**Given:** hypotenuse 17 in, one leg 8 in. **Method:** a leg is missing, so subtract the squares. b² = 17² − 8² = 289 − 64 = 225; b = √225 = **15 in**. The answer being shorter than the hypotenuse is the first check, and it passes.

### Example 3: Solve a 30-60-90 Triangle

**Given:** short leg 5 cm. **Method:** the ratio 1 : √3 : 2 does the work, no theorem needed. Longer leg = 5√3 ≈ **8.66 cm**; hypotenuse = 2 × 5 = **10 cm**.

## Common Mistakes in Right Triangle Worksheets

**Identifying the wrong hypotenuse.** Wrong: treating a leg as c because it is drawn diagonally in a rotated diagram. Correct: the hypotenuse is defined by position, opposite the right angle, never by how the drawing happens to sit.

**Using the wrong theorem direction.** Wrong: finding a missing leg with b = √(c² + a²), which produces a "leg" longer than the hypotenuse. Correct: missing leg subtracts, b = √(c² − a²); missing hypotenuse adds.

**Forgetting the square root.** Wrong: legs 3 and 4, hypotenuse = 9 + 16 = 25. That 25 is c², not c. Correct: c = √25 = 5.

**Mixing perimeter and area.** Wrong: answering "area = 6 + 8 + 10 = 24 cm" for a 6-8-10 triangle. That sum is the perimeter. Correct: area = ½ × 6 × 8 = 24 cm²; same digits here, different quantity, different units.

**Confusing the special ratios.** Wrong: giving a 30-60-90 with short leg 5 a long leg of 5√2. The √2 belongs to the 45-45-90. Correct: long leg = 5√3, hypotenuse 10.

**Rounding too early.** Wrong: √74 rounded to 8.6 at the first step, then squared or added onward. Correct: keep √74 exact through the work and round the final answer, 8.60, once.

**Choosing the wrong trig ratio.** Wrong: using tangent when the hypotenuse is the known side. Tangent connects the two legs only. Correct: if the hypotenuse is in the problem, the ratio is sine or cosine; pick by whether the target side faces the angle (sine) or touches it (cosine).
