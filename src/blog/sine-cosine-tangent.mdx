## What Are Sine, Cosine, and Tangent?

Sine, cosine, and tangent are trigonometric ratios that measure the relationship between the angles of a right triangle and the lengths of its sides. For any given angle, these ratios remain constant, allowing you to calculate unknown side lengths or missing angles when only partial information is available.

### Definition and Purpose
In a right triangle, the ratio of any two sides depends entirely on the triangle's acute angles. 
- **Sine (sin):** The ratio of the opposite side to the hypotenuse.
- **Cosine (cos):** The ratio of the adjacent side to the hypotenuse.
- **Tangent (tan):** The ratio of the opposite side to the adjacent side.

### Why Do These Ratios Exist?
If you take a right triangle and scale it up to be 100 times larger, the angles inside remain exactly the same. Because the shape remains proportional, the ratio of its sides remains identical. Early mathematicians (dating back to ancient astronomy) realized they could create a universal "cheat sheet" (trigonometric tables) mapping every possible angle to its specific side ratios. 

### Comparison Table: Trigonometric Ratios

| Ratio Name | Abbreviation | Formula (Ratio) | Primary Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sine** | sin(θ) | Opposite / Hypotenuse | Finding vertical heights or hypotenuse |
| **Cosine** | cos(θ) | Adjacent / Hypotenuse | Finding horizontal distances or hypotenuse |
| **Tangent** | tan(θ) | Opposite / Adjacent | Finding slopes, pitches, and heights without hypotenuse |

> **Quick Tip:** The symbol **θ** (theta) is a Greek letter universally used in math to represent an unknown angle.

## Understanding Opposite, Adjacent, and Hypotenuse

To use sine, cosine, and tangent, you must correctly identify the Hypotenuse, the Opposite side, and the Adjacent side. The hypotenuse never changes, but the "opposite" and "adjacent" sides swap depending on which acute angle you are looking at.

### How to Identify Them
1. **Hypotenuse:** Always the longest side, strictly located across from the 90-degree right angle.
2. **Opposite Side:** The leg located directly across from the specific angle (θ) you are focusing on. It does not touch the angle at all.
3. **Adjacent Side:** The leg that forms the angle (θ) alongside the hypotenuse. It "touches" the angle but is not the longest side.

### How the Reference Angle Decides Opposite vs Adjacent
The reference angle is simply the angle you plug into the formula. The side across from it (not touching it at all) is always the **opposite** side. The side that touches it, besides the hypotenuse, is always the **adjacent** side. Switch to the other acute angle and the two labels switch: what was opposite becomes adjacent, and what was adjacent becomes opposite. The hypotenuse never changes.

Think of it this way: if you stand at Angle A, the wall across the room is the "Opposite" side. If you walk across the room and stand at Angle B, that same wall is now right next to you, so it has become the "Adjacent" side. **The labels are relative to your reference angle.**

### Decision Chart: Labeling Sides

| Step | Action | Identification |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Find the 90° corner and look directly across it. | **Hypotenuse** |
| 2 | Pick your target angle (θ). Look directly across from it. | **Opposite Side** |
| 3 | Find the remaining side touching the angle. | **Adjacent Side** |

## What Is SOHCAHTOA?

SOHCAHTOA is a universally recognized mathematical mnemonic used to memorize the formulas for sine, cosine, and tangent. It stands for: **S**ine is **O**pposite over **H**ypotenuse, **C**osine is **A**djacent over **H**ypotenuse, and **T**angent is **O**pposite over **A**djacent.

### Meaning of Every Letter
- **S O H:** **S**in(θ) = **O**pposite / **H**ypotenuse
- **C A H:** **C**os(θ) = **A**djacent / **H**ypotenuse
- **T O A:** **T**an(θ) = **O**pposite / **A**djacent

### Visual Explanation and Memory Tricks
If SOHCAHTOA is hard to remember, many students use silly phrases where the first letter of each word spells out the mnemonic:
- **S**ome **O**ld **H**orses **C**an **A**lways **H**ear **T**heir **O**wners **A**pproach.
- **S**ome **O**f **H**er **C**ats **A**re **H**aving **T**rouble **O**ver **A**pples.

### SOHCAHTOA Cheat Sheet

| Mnemonic Part | Function | Equation Format |
|---|---|---|
| **SOH** | Sine | sin(θ) = O / H |
| **CAH** | Cosine | cos(θ) = A / H |
| **TOA** | Tangent | tan(θ) = O / A |

## Sine Formula Explained

The sine formula is defined as the ratio of the side opposite the angle to the hypotenuse. The equation is written as **sin(θ) = Opposite / Hypotenuse**. It is primarily used when dealing with the height of a triangle and its longest side.

### When to Use Sine
Use sine when you know (or need to find) the **Opposite** side and the **Hypotenuse**. 

### When NOT to Use Sine
Do not use sine if the problem involves the Adjacent side and does not provide the Hypotenuse.

### How it Works (Worked Example)
Imagine a right triangle where the angle is 30° and the Hypotenuse is 10. You need the Opposite side.
- **Formula:** sin(30°) = Opp / 10
- **Step 1:** Calculate sin(30°) on a calculator, which is exactly 0.5.
- **Step 2:** 0.5 = Opp / 10.
- **Step 3:** Multiply both sides by 10. Opp = 5.

### Common Mistake
A frequent error is dividing the Hypotenuse by the Opposite side instead of the other way around. The formula is strictly **O divided by H**.

---

## Cosine Formula Explained

The cosine formula is defined as the ratio of the side adjacent to the angle to the hypotenuse. The equation is **cos(θ) = Adjacent / Hypotenuse**. It is most commonly used for calculating horizontal distances along the ground.

### Applications and Meaning
While sine often relates to vertical height (the y-axis on a coordinate plane), cosine relates to horizontal distance (the x-axis). When a ladder leans against a wall, cosine helps you find how far the base of the ladder is from the wall.

### Calculator Walkthrough
To find the adjacent side when Angle = 60° and Hypotenuse = 20:
- **Formula:** cos(60°) = Adj / 20
- **Step 1:** Press `cos`, type `60`, and press `=`. Ensure your calculator is in "Degree" mode. The result is 0.5.
- **Step 2:** 0.5 = Adj / 20.
- **Step 3:** Adj = 20 × 0.5 = 10.

### Common Mistake
Using cosine when the angle provided is the top angle, but treating the ground distance as the adjacent side. Remember, the adjacent side must *touch* the specific angle you are using.

---

## Tangent Formula Explained

The tangent formula is defined as the ratio of the opposite side to the adjacent side. The equation is **tan(θ) = Opposite / Adjacent**. Because it does not use the hypotenuse, it is perfectly suited for finding slope, roof pitch, or the height of a building from the ground.

### Slope Relationship
Tangent is identical to the concept of "Rise over Run" in algebra. The Opposite side is the "Rise" (vertical), and the Adjacent side is the "Run" (horizontal). Therefore, the tangent of an angle gives you the exact slope of the hypotenuse line.

### How it Works (Worked Example)
You are 50 feet away from a tree (Adjacent = 50). You look up at a 45° angle to see the top (θ = 45°). How tall is the tree (Opposite)?
- **Formula:** tan(45°) = Opp / 50
- **Step 1:** tan(45°) = 1.
- **Step 2:** 1 = Opp / 50.
- **Step 3:** Opp = 50 feet.

## How to Choose Between Sine, Cosine, and Tangent

Choosing the correct ratio is a process of elimination: label your sides relative to the reference angle, note which side you know and which you need, then match the pair to SOHCAHTOA. If the pair includes the hypotenuse, use sine (opposite) or cosine (adjacent); if it is the two legs only, use tangent.

## Complete Worked Examples

Here are 30 original worked examples demonstrating how to apply these formulas across various difficulties and real-world scenarios.

### Beginner Examples (Finding Missing Sides)
1. **Given:** Angle=30°, Hyp=12. **Find:** Opp. 
   - **Formula:** sin(30°) = Opp/12 ➔ 0.5 = Opp/12 ➔ Opp = 6.
2. **Given:** Angle=60°, Hyp=10. **Find:** Adj.
   - **Formula:** cos(60°) = Adj/10 ➔ 0.5 = Adj/10 ➔ Adj = 5.
3. **Given:** Angle=45°, Adj=8. **Find:** Opp.
   - **Formula:** tan(45°) = Opp/8 ➔ 1 = Opp/8 ➔ Opp = 8.
4. **Given:** Angle=20°, Opp=5. **Find:** Hyp.
   - **Formula:** sin(20°) = 5/Hyp ➔ 0.342 = 5/Hyp ➔ Hyp = 5 / 0.342 ≈ 14.62.
5. **Given:** Angle=50°, Adj=15. **Find:** Hyp.
   - **Formula:** cos(50°) = 15/Hyp ➔ 0.6428 = 15/Hyp ➔ Hyp = 15 / 0.6428 ≈ 23.34.

### Intermediate Examples (Using Inverse Functions for Angles)
*Note: To find an angle, use the inverse function on your calculator (sin⁻¹, cos⁻¹, or tan⁻¹).*
6. **Given:** Opp=3, Hyp=5. **Find:** Angle.
   - **Formula:** sin(θ) = 3/5 = 0.6 ➔ θ = sin⁻¹(0.6) ≈ 36.87°.
7. **Given:** Adj=4, Hyp=5. **Find:** Angle.
   - **Formula:** cos(θ) = 4/5 = 0.8 ➔ θ = cos⁻¹(0.8) ≈ 36.87°.
8. **Given:** Opp=5, Adj=12. **Find:** Angle.
   - **Formula:** tan(θ) = 5/12 ≈ 0.4167 ➔ θ = tan⁻¹(5/12) ≈ 22.62°.
9. **Given:** Opp=10, Hyp=20. **Find:** Angle.
   - **Formula:** sin(θ) = 10/20 = 0.5 ➔ θ = sin⁻¹(0.5) = 30°.
10. **Given:** Opp=7, Adj=7. **Find:** Angle.
    - **Formula:** tan(θ) = 7/7 = 1 ➔ θ = tan⁻¹(1) = 45°.

### Advanced & Real-World Examples
11. **Construction (Ladder):** A 20ft ladder leans against a wall at a 75° angle to the ground. How high up the wall does it reach?
    - **Given:** Hyp=20, Angle=75°. **Find:** Opp.
    - **Solve:** sin(75°) = Opp/20 ➔ 0.9659 = Opp/20 ➔ Opp ≈ 19.3ft.
12. **Architecture (Roof Pitch):** A roof rises 6ft (Opp) over a horizontal run of 12ft (Adj). What is the roof angle?
    - **Solve:** tan(θ) = 6/12 = 0.5 ➔ θ = tan⁻¹(0.5) ≈ 26.56°.
13. **Surveying:** You stand 100m from a building (Adj) and look up at a 40° angle. How tall is it?
    - **Solve:** tan(40°) = Opp/100 ➔ 0.839 = Opp/100 ➔ Opp = 83.9m.
14. **Aviation:** A plane descends at a 3° angle. If the ground distance to the runway is 5 miles (Adj), what is the plane's altitude (Opp)?
    - **Solve:** tan(3°) = Opp/5 ➔ 0.0524 = Opp/5 ➔ Opp ≈ 0.262 miles (1,383 feet).
15. **Computer Graphics:** A ray is cast at a 45° angle. Its x-coordinate (Adj) is 50. Find the y-coordinate (Opp).
    - **Solve:** tan(45°) = Opp/50 ➔ 1 = Opp/50 ➔ Opp = 50.
16. **Physics:** A force of 100N (Hyp) is applied at a 30° angle. Find the horizontal component (Adj).
    - **Solve:** cos(30°) = Adj/100 ➔ 0.866 = Adj/100 ➔ Adj = 86.6N.
17. **Navigation:** A boat travels 10 miles. Its compass bearing forms a 25° angle with the shoreline. How far off the shoreline (Opp) is it?
    - **Solve:** sin(25°) = Opp/10 ➔ 0.4226 = Opp/10 ➔ Opp ≈ 4.22 miles.
18. **Engineering (Bridge):** A support cable forms a 60° angle with the bridge deck and attaches 30m away from the tower (Adj). Cable length (Hyp)?
    - **Solve:** cos(60°) = 30/Hyp ➔ 0.5 = 30/Hyp ➔ Hyp = 60m.
19. **Video Game Dev:** A camera looks down at a character standing 10 units away horizontally. The angle of depression measured from the horizontal is 45°. What is the camera height?
    - **Setup:** The angle is measured from the horizontal, so the horizontal distance (10 units) is adjacent to the angle, and the camera height is opposite to the angle.
    - **Solve:** tan(45°) = Opp/Adj ➔ 1 = height/10 ➔ height = 10 units.
20. **Astronomy:** Observing a crater on the moon, shadows form a right triangle. Angle of sun is 15°, shadow length (Adj) is 2km. Crater depth (Opp)?
    - **Solve:** tan(15°) = Opp/2 ➔ 0.2679 = Opp/2 ➔ Opp ≈ 0.53km.

### Advanced & Real-World Examples (continued)
21. **Robotics (Arm Reach):** A robot arm extends 12cm horizontally (Adj) from its joint at a 60° angle of elevation. How high (Opp) does the gripper reach?
    - **Solve:** tan(60°) = Opp/12 ➔ 1.7321 = Opp/12 ➔ Opp ≈ 20.78cm.
22. **Kite Flying:** A kite string 50m long (Hyp) makes a 55° angle with the ground. How high is the kite (Opp)?
    - **Solve:** sin(55°) = Opp/50 ➔ 0.8192 = Opp/50 ➔ Opp ≈ 40.96m.
23. **Wheelchair Ramp:** A ramp must rise 1.5m (Opp) at an angle no steeper than 4.8°. How long must the ramp surface (Hyp) be?
    - **Solve:** sin(4.8°) = 1.5/Hyp ➔ 0.0837 = 1.5/Hyp ➔ Hyp = 1.5 / 0.0837 ≈ 17.93m.
24. **Staircase:** A staircase rises at a 35° angle with a horizontal run of 4m (Adj). What is the total rise (Opp)?
    - **Solve:** tan(35°) = Opp/4 ➔ 0.7002 = Opp/4 ➔ Opp ≈ 2.80m.
25. **Shadows (Flagpole):** A flagpole casts a 9m shadow (Adj) when the sun is at a 62° angle of elevation. How tall is the flagpole (Opp)?
    - **Solve:** tan(62°) = Opp/9 ➔ 1.8807 = Opp/9 ➔ Opp ≈ 16.93m.
26. **Ladder Angle:** A 12ft ladder stands with its base 4ft (Adj) from the wall. What angle does it make with the ground?
    - **Solve:** cos(θ) = 4/12 ≈ 0.3333 ➔ θ = cos⁻¹(0.3333) ≈ 70.53°.
27. **Roof Truss:** A roof truss spans 14m total, so each rafter covers a 7m horizontal half-span (Adj) at a 30° pitch. How long is each rafter (Hyp)?
    - **Solve:** cos(30°) = 7/Hyp ➔ 0.8660 = 7/Hyp ➔ Hyp = 7 / 0.8660 ≈ 8.08m.
28. **Road Grade:** A road has a 6% grade (6m of rise per 100m of run). What is the road's angle?
    - **Solve:** tan(θ) = 6/100 = 0.06 ➔ θ = tan⁻¹(0.06) ≈ 3.43°.
29. **Hot-Air Balloon:** You stand 200m (Adj) from the point directly below a balloon and measure a 38° angle of elevation. How high is the balloon (Opp)?
    - **Solve:** tan(38°) = Opp/200 ➔ 0.7813 = Opp/200 ➔ Opp ≈ 156.26m.
30. **Ship Navigation:** From your boat you measure a 20° angle of elevation to the top of a 30m lighthouse mast (Opp). How far away (Adj) are you?
    - **Solve:** tan(20°) = 30/Adj ➔ 0.3640 = 30/Adj ➔ Adj = 30 / 0.3640 ≈ 82.42m.

## Common Mistakes

Here are 25 mistakes students make and how to avoid them:

1. **Calculator in Radians:** Getting negative/wrong answers because the calculator is not in 'Degrees' mode. **Fix:** Always check the 'DEG' symbol on your screen.
2. **Flipping the Ratio:** Writing sin(θ) = Hyp/Opp. **Fix:** Remember SOH; Sine is Opp over Hyp.
3. **Using Inverse Trig for Sides:** Pressing `sin⁻¹` when trying to find a side length. **Fix:** Inverse trig is *only* for finding missing angles.
4. **Using Sine instead of Tangent:** Guessing a formula instead of labeling sides.
5. **Treating the Hypotenuse as Adjacent:** The hypotenuse touches the angle, so students label it 'Adjacent'. **Fix:** The longest side is strictly the hypotenuse; the *other* touching side is adjacent.
6. **Solving tan(θ) = 5/x incorrectly:** Writing x = 5 * tan(θ). **Fix:** Use algebra correctly: x = 5 / tan(θ).
7. **Rounding too early:** Rounding sin(25°) to 0.4 and using it for the rest of the problem. **Fix:** Leave the full decimal in the calculator until the very end.
8. **Applying SOHCAHTOA to non-right triangles:** **Fix:** SOHCAHTOA only works if there is a 90° angle.

9. **Labeling sides before picking the reference angle:** Assigning opposite and adjacent first, then choosing the angle. **Fix:** Pick the reference angle first, then label the sides relative to it.
10. **Using the 90° angle as θ:** Plugging 90° into sin, cos, or tan. **Fix:** SOHCAHTOA only uses the two acute angles; sin(90°) = 1 and tan(90°) is undefined.
11. **Dividing in the wrong order when solving for the hypotenuse:** Writing Hyp = sin(θ) / known side instead of known side / sin(θ). **Fix:** Rearrange properly: if sin(θ) = Opp/Hyp, then Hyp = Opp / sin(θ).
12. **Missing parentheses on the calculator:** Typing `sin 30/2` and getting sin(30)/2 instead of sin(15). **Fix:** Type the angle computation in parentheses: `sin(30/2)`.
13. **Assuming tan(θ) > 1 means an error:** Seeing 1.88 and retyping the problem. **Fix:** Tangent routinely exceeds 1; it only means the opposite side is longer than the adjacent side.
14. **Trusting a rotated drawing:** Labeling the side drawn vertically as "opposite" regardless of the reference angle. **Fix:** Labels come from the angle, never from the drawing's orientation.
15. **Skipping the sanity check:** Accepting a hypotenuse shorter than a leg. **Fix:** The hypotenuse must be the longest side; if it is not, recheck the labels.
16. **Getting the angle instead of the side (and vice versa):** Answering 0.6 when asked for the angle. **Fix:** A ratio is not an angle; press the inverse function (sin⁻¹) to convert a ratio into degrees.
17. **Feeding radians into a degree problem:** Using a radian-mode calculator on a problem stated in degrees. **Fix:** Same as mistake 1, but check it again after every calculator reset, since many calculators default back to radians.
18. **Plugging the hypotenuse value into the opposite slot:** Copying 10 (the hypotenuse) into the formula as the opposite side. **Fix:** Match each number to its labeled side before substituting.
19. **Checking work with the same ratio:** Recomputing sin after solving with sin. **Fix:** Verify with a different tool, such as the Pythagorean theorem or a second ratio.
20. **Rounding the final angle too aggressively:** Reporting 37° when the working needs 36.87°. **Fix:** Keep two decimal places unless the problem says otherwise.
21. **Forgetting the two acute angles sum to 90°:** Finishing with angles of 40° and 60°. **Fix:** In a right triangle the acute angles are complementary; use this as a free check.
22. **Treating tan(90°) as a number:** Trying to compute it when the "angle" approaches the right angle. **Fix:** Tangent is undefined at 90°; your reference angle must be acute.
23. **Mixing units in word problems:** Using feet for one side and inches for another. **Fix:** Convert every measurement to the same unit before substituting.
24. **Stopping mid-algebra:** Leaving the answer as "Opp/20 = 0.9659" without multiplying. **Fix:** Finish the rearrangement and state the final side length with units.
25. **Forgetting to switch calculator modes between problems:** Solving a degree problem right after a radian one. **Fix:** Glance at the DEG/RAD indicator before every calculation.
